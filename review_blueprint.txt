Review Blueprint

Working Title
Automatic Speech Recognition for Low-Resource Languages in the Foundation-Model Era: Resources, Adaptation, Evaluation, and Multimodal Robustness

Target Journal
Artificial Intelligence Review

Review Type
Systematic mapping review + critical taxonomy

Main Objective
To provide a field-level synthesis of low-resource automatic speech recognition in the foundation-model era, with emphasis on resources, model adaptation, data-centric methods, pseudo-label reliability, evaluation practice, and multimodal robustness.

Core Argument
Foundation models have changed the starting point of low-resource ASR, but they have not solved low-resource ASR. Persistent bottlenecks include data quality, language and dialect mismatch, orthographic normalization, adaptation strategy, pseudo-label reliability, fair evaluation, reproducibility, compute cost, and robustness under noisy or multimodal conditions.

Positioning
This paper should not be framed as a Pashto review or as a thesis summary. Pashto may be used as a selective illustrative example of broader low-resource ASR problems such as script normalization, dialect variation, telephony noise, pseudo-label reliability, and multimodal data construction.

Primary Audience
Researchers working on speech recognition, speech foundation models, low-resource NLP/speech, multilingual transfer, speech technology for underrepresented languages, multimodal learning, and trustworthy AI evaluation.

Review Scope
The review covers:
1. Evolution from hybrid ASR to end-to-end ASR and foundation speech models.
2. Definitions and dimensions of low-resource ASR beyond labeled hours.
3. Datasets and benchmarks for low-resource and multilingual ASR.
4. Self-supervised and multilingual representation learning.
5. Weakly supervised and foundation ASR models.
6. Adaptation methods, including fine-tuning, continued pretraining, adapters, LoRA/QLoRA, prompt-based adaptation, and transfer learning.
7. Data-centric methods, including transcript normalization, filtering, augmentation, pseudo-labeling, and knowledge distillation.
8. Evaluation practice, including WER, CER, dialect-wise evaluation, domain-wise evaluation, noise robustness, fairness, reproducibility, and deployment constraints.
9. Multimodal and future directions, including AVSR, lip-reading, visual speech recognition, speech-language models, LLM-assisted correction, and LLM rescoring.

Expected Contributions
1. A modern taxonomy of low-resource ASR in the foundation-model era.
2. A synthesis of data-centric and model-centric solutions.
3. A critical analysis of adaptation, pseudo-labeling, and knowledge distillation.
4. A discussion of evaluation gaps, including dialect, domain, robustness, fairness, reproducibility, and compute cost.
5. A future research agenda for multimodal, LLM-assisted, and reliable low-resource ASR.

Review Questions
RQ1. What does “low-resource” mean in ASR beyond limited labeled speech?
RQ2. How have foundation speech models changed the starting point for low-resource ASR?
RQ3. Which data-centric and model-centric strategies are used to adapt ASR systems to low-resource languages?
RQ4. How are pseudo-labeling and knowledge distillation used in low-resource ASR, and what reliability problems remain?
RQ5. How should low-resource ASR systems be evaluated beyond global WER/CER?
RQ6. What future role will multimodal and LLM-assisted approaches play in robust low-resource ASR?

Proposed Manuscript Structure
1. Introduction
2. Review methodology and search protocol
3. From hybrid ASR to foundation speech models
4. What makes ASR low-resource?
5. Resources and benchmarks
6. Model-centric approaches
7. Adaptation strategies
8. Data-centric approaches
9. Evaluation and reproducibility
10. Multimodal and LLM-assisted low-resource ASR
11. Taxonomy, comparative synthesis, and gap analysis
12. Future research agenda
13. Conclusion

Style Requirements
The review should be analytical, critical, and constructive. It should avoid exaggerated claims, avoid unsupported performance comparisons, and distinguish evidence from interpretation. It should use polished academic English suitable for a Q1 review journal.
