Working Title
Automatic Speech Recognition for Low-Resource Languages in the Foundation-Model Era: Resources, Adaptation, Evaluation, and Multimodal Robustness

Target Journal
Artificial Intelligence Review

Review Type
Systematic mapping review + critical taxonomy

Main Review Question
How have foundation speech models changed low-resource ASR, and what resource, adaptation, evaluation, and robustness challenges still prevent reliable recognition for underrepresented languages?

Main Objective
To provide a field-level synthesis of low-resource automatic speech recognition in the foundation-model era, organized around the six locked review questions in 01_scope_and_planning/research_questions.md.

Backbone Statement
This review examines how foundation speech models have reshaped low-resource ASR, while arguing that reliable recognition for underrepresented languages still depends on resource quality, language-aware adaptation, pseudo-label reliability, fair evaluation, reproducibility, and robustness under noisy or multimodal conditions.

Positioning
This paper is not a Pashto review and is not a thesis summary. Pashto may be used selectively as an illustrative example of broader low-resource ASR issues such as normalization, dialect imbalance, pseudo-label quality, noisy speech, and evaluation.

Locked Review Questions
RQ1. What does "low-resource" mean in ASR beyond limited labeled hours?
RQ2. How have self-supervised, weakly supervised, multilingual, and foundation speech models changed low-resource ASR?
RQ3. Which data-centric strategies remain necessary in the foundation-model era?
RQ4. Which adaptation strategies are most effective for low-resource ASR, and under what conditions?
RQ5. How reliable are pseudo-labeling and knowledge distillation for low-resource ASR?
RQ6. How should low-resource ASR be evaluated and extended toward robust, multimodal, and LLM-assisted systems?

Contribution Structure
1. A foundation-model-era taxonomy of low-resource ASR, organizing the field by resource condition, language condition, model family, adaptation method, supervision strategy, and evaluation setting.
2. A synthesis of data-centric and model-centric solutions, showing how corpus quality, normalization, filtering, augmentation, SSL, multilingual transfer, and foundation models interact.
3. A critical review of adaptation, pseudo-labeling, and knowledge distillation, focusing on parameter-efficient tuning, continued pretraining, teacher reliability, confidence filtering, and multi-teacher disagreement.
4. A future research agenda for reliable, multimodal, and LLM-assisted low-resource ASR, covering dialect-aware evaluation, reproducible benchmarking, compute-efficient adaptation, multimodal and audio-visual robustness, and cautious LLM-assisted correction, rescoring, contextual biasing, and post-ASR normalization.

Six-Layer Taxonomy
1. Resource layer: labeled, unlabeled, weakly labeled, pseudo-labeled, multilingual, multimodal.
2. Language layer: dialect variation, orthographic inconsistency, code-switching, morphology, script complexity.
3. Model layer: hybrid ASR, E2E ASR, SSL models, multilingual ASR, weakly supervised ASR, speech foundation models, speech-LLM systems.
4. Adaptation layer: fine-tuning, continued pretraining, adapters, LoRA/QLoRA, prompting, transfer learning.
5. Supervision layer: supervised learning, SSL, self-training, pseudo-labeling, single-teacher KD, multi-teacher KD.
6. Evaluation layer: WER/CER, dialect-wise, domain-wise, noise robustness, reproducibility, compute/deployment cost, hallucination and over-correction checks for LLM-assisted ASR.

Style Requirements
The review should be analytical, critical, and constructive. It should avoid exaggerated claims, avoid unsupported performance comparisons, distinguish evidence from interpretation, and use polished academic English suitable for a Q1 review journal.
