# Table 6. Future Research Agenda and Reporting Checklist

Purpose: Manuscript-ready draft table for Section 13. Source basis: `07_draft_sections/13_future_research_agenda.md`, `05_synthesis_matrices/block_g_research_gap_agenda_matrix.csv`, `05_synthesis_matrices/block_g_core_claim_synthesis_map.csv`, `05_synthesis_matrices/block_g_additional_resource_candidates.csv`, and `08_tables/table_05_evaluation_robustness_checklist.md`.

| Agenda area | What future studies should report | Why it matters | Linked gaps | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-resource definition and resource conditions | Labeled data, unlabeled data, weak labels, validation data, transcript policy, compute constraints, target language or variety, and deployment setting | Clear definitions prevent incomparable claims that use "low-resource" for different combinations of scarcity, quality, validation, and compute | GAP-G1; GAP-G2; GAP-G12 | [@besacier2014_underresourced_asr_survey; @peterson2022_openasr21; @chen2025mlsuperb2challenge] |
| Dataset documentation, metadata, provenance, consent/licensing, and access | Data source, collection process, validation protocol, speaker and dialect metadata where ethical, licensing, access limits, and known corpus risks | Dataset size alone does not show whether a corpus supports reliable ASR or fair benchmark interpretation | GAP-G2; GAP-G4; GAP-G12 | [@ardila2020commonvoice; @lau2025_data_quality_multilingual_speech; @gebru2021_datasheets_for_datasets; @bender2018_data_statements] |
| Orthography, script, transcript normalization, and scoring rules | Tokenization, punctuation, casing, diacritics, spacing, script handling, raw and normalized scoring, and public scoring scripts where possible | Scoring differences can make systems look better or worse without reflecting recognition quality | GAP-G3; GAP-G9 | [@karita2023lenient; @thennal2024cer; @lau2025_data_quality_multilingual_speech] |
| Dialect, accent, language-variety, domain, and channel coverage | Language-wise, variety-wise, accent-wise, domain-wise, and channel-wise coverage plus averaging policy and metadata availability | Aggregate scores can hide failures for the communities, domains, or recording conditions that motivate low-resource ASR | GAP-G4; GAP-G10 | [@shi2023mlsuperb; @chen2025mlsuperb2challenge; @olatunji2023_afrispeech200; @koenecke2020racialdisparities] |
| Compute-efficient and forgetting-aware adaptation | Base checkpoint, adaptation data, trainable parameters, memory, hardware, training cost, inference cost, and prior-language retention tests | Adaptation claims are not comparable unless accuracy, compute, and forgetting are reported together | GAP-G5; GAP-G6; GAP-G12 | [@getman24_interspeech; @udupa24_interspeech; @qian24_interspeech; @feng2022superbslt] |
| Pseudo-labeling and KD reliability controls | Teacher identity, decoding settings, confidence or uncertainty rules, thresholds, retained labels where known, teacher agreement, and validation behavior | Generated supervision can scale teacher errors unless filtering, disagreement, and target-domain validation are transparent | GAP-G7; GAP-G8 | [@park20d_interspeech; @khurana2020dust; @leal21_interspeech; @bhogale24_interspeech] |
| Evaluation beyond aggregate WER/CER | Pooled and macro scores, language/dialect/domain breakdowns, normalization policy, robustness tests, and evaluation split construction | WER/CER are necessary but insufficient when hidden preprocessing or pooling hides low-resource failures | GAP-G3; GAP-G4; GAP-G9 | [@shi2023mlsuperb; @conneau2022fleurs; @conneau2022xtreme_s; @karita2023lenient] |
| Robustness under noise, far-field, overlap, and conversation | Noise, overlap, far-field, diarization-aware, channel-shift, and conversational protocols where relevant | Clean read-speech performance does not establish deployment reliability in noisy or multi-speaker settings | GAP-G4; GAP-G12 | [@watanabe2020chime6; @cornell2023chime7] |
| Multimodal/AVSR resource and evaluation protocols | Modality availability, alignment, audio-only and audio-visual results, missing-modality tests, privacy constraints, and language/domain coverage | AVSR may help degraded audio but introduces modality, coverage, privacy, and comparability risks | GAP-G10; GAP-G12 | [@shi2022avhubert; @shi2022robustavsr; @anwar23_interspeech] |
| Constrained LLM-assisted correction/rescoring | N-best or lattice grounding, validated context, correction constraints, named-entity handling, hallucination checks, and over-correction tests | Unconstrained rewriting can produce fluent but unsupported transcripts or conceal recognition errors | GAP-G9; GAP-G11 | [@koenecke2024carelesswhisper; @ma2024asrerrorcorrection; @koudounas2025shallow; @wang2025contextasrbench] |
| Reproducibility, compute, memory, latency, and deployment reporting | Code or recipe availability, model version, data splits, scoring scripts, hardware, memory, latency, training and inference cost, and data access constraints | Low-resource progress is difficult to compare or deploy when results depend on unreproducible or unaffordable settings | GAP-G5; GAP-G6; GAP-G12 | [@watanabe2018espnet; @ravanelli2021speechbrain; @povey2011kaldi; @strubell2019_energy_policy] |
| Community-aware resource development and language expertise | Local language expertise, community consultation where appropriate, consent/licensing, dialect names, orthographic conventions, intended use, and known exclusions | Resource design can miss real language use when metadata and community knowledge are absent | GAP-G1; GAP-G2; GAP-G3 | [@ardila2020commonvoice; @bender2018_data_statements; @gebru2021_datasheets_for_datasets; @mitchell2019_model_cards] |
