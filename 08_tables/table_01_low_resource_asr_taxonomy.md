# Table 1. Low-Resource ASR Taxonomy

Purpose: Manuscript-ready draft table for Section 6. Source basis: `05_synthesis_matrices/block_g_cross_block_taxonomy_synthesis_matrix.csv`, especially the six-layer taxonomy rows and their residual-risk fields.

| Taxonomy layer | Subdimension | What it captures | Typical low-resource risk | Review sections |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resource | Labeled speech, unlabeled speech, weakly labeled/web-mined data, and pseudo-labeled supervision | The available supervision sources for training, adaptation, validation, and evaluation | Labeled hours may be narrow; unlabeled data lack task validation; weak labels and pseudo-labels can import source noise or teacher errors | 3, 5, 7, 9, 12 |
| Resource | Multilingual corpora and multimodal/audio-visual resources | Cross-language transfer evidence and robustness-oriented data beyond audio-only transcripts | Language presence can hide dialect or domain absence; AVSR evidence may be concentrated by language, domain, or recording condition | 5, 6, 11, 13 |
| Language | Dialect, accent, orthography, script, morphology, and code-switching | Linguistic conditions that shape acoustic modeling, transcript comparability, tokenization, and scoring | Missing metadata or inconsistent normalization can make results appear comparable when they are not | 3, 5, 7, 10, 12 |
| Language | Language-family or typological mismatch | Transfer distance between target languages and pretraining or donor-language evidence | Aggregate multilingual results may hide poor transfer to distant or underrepresented target languages | 4, 6, 8, 10 |
| Model | Hybrid ASR, end-to-end ASR, SSL encoders, multilingual SSL, weakly supervised ASR, SpeechLMs, and multimodal foundation systems | The model family and its assumptions about labels, transfer, generation, and modality | Model scale or language coverage may be mistaken for target-language reliability | 4, 6, 11, 12 |
| Adaptation | Full fine-tuning, continued pretraining, adapters, LoRA/QLoRA, transfer, prompting or contextual biasing, and forgetting control | How a pretrained system is specialized to a target language, domain, or deployment setting | Comparisons can be misleading if data volume, compute, trainable parameters, and forgetting are not reported | 8, 11, 13 |
| Supervision | Supervised learning, self-supervised learning, self-training, pseudo-label filtering, single-teacher KD, multi-teacher KD, and agreement-based selection | How human labels, unlabeled speech, teacher outputs, and teacher agreement shape learning signals | Generated supervision may scale errors when teacher quality, confidence, uncertainty, and disagreement are hidden | 4, 9, 12, 13 |
| Evaluation | WER/CER, orthography-aware scoring, dialect/domain breakdowns, robustness, fairness, reproducibility, compute, AVSR, and LLM-assisted ASR evaluation | How evidence is measured, compared, reproduced, and bounded for deployment-facing claims | Pooled WER/CER can hide language, dialect, domain, script, demographic, hallucination, and compute-related failures | 10, 11, 12, 13 |

Table note: Representative evidence for the taxonomy is distributed across Sections 3-13 and the Core 60 evidence base; rows synthesize Block G taxonomy entries rather than single papers. Compact anchors for the main layers include low-resource framing and resources [@besacier2014_underresourced_asr_survey; @ardila2020commonvoice], foundation and multilingual speech models [@babu22_interspeech; @pmlr-v202-radford23a; @pratap2024mms], adaptation and supervision controls [@getman24_interspeech; @udupa24_interspeech; @park20d_interspeech; @leal21_interspeech], and evaluation/robustness controls [@shi2023mlsuperb; @karita2023lenient; @watanabe2020chime6; @koenecke2024carelesswhisper].
